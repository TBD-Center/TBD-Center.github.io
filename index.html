About

About the TBD Hub

The TBD Hub is the Travel Behavior Data Hub of the National Center for Understanding Future Travel Behavior and Demand (TBD), a University Transportation Center funded by the U.S. Department of Transportation. It brings travel behavior dashboards into one place: a one-stop shop for data-driven insights on travel behavior and demand.

The vision

The TBD Hub is being built through a multi-year, multi-university effort that spans several disciplines and research thrusts. Over time it will bring together data sets such as the American Community Survey (ACS), National Household Travel Survey (NHTS), American Time Use Survey (ATUS), Consumer Expenditure Survey (CES), Commodity Flow Survey (CFS), Vehicle Inventory and Use Survey (VIUS), National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES), Residential Energy Consumption Survey (RECS), Commercial Buildings Energy Consumption Survey (CBECS) and Air Quality (AirNow) data, along with aggregated origin-destination flow data from partners such as StreetLight Data.

The team draws on the latest techniques in data aggregation, fusion and integration, imputation and weighting, and machine learning prediction. Alongside tabulations, charts and dynamic visualizations, the hub will include a well-being calculator, an energy footprint calculator and a mobility poverty calculator to support planning for accessibility, sustainability and community well-being. More details are on the TBD Hub research project page.

What you can do here

  • Browse TBD dashboards and external dashboards on travel behavior and demand.
  • Search topics and survey variables across the TBD dashboards (external dashboards have their own search in their section). Results show matching dashboards first, then each matching variable with the dashboards that contain it, the section and variable name, and a link to the page where it is shown.
  • Ask to list your own dashboard.

Where the search comes from

The search index is built from each dashboard's data dictionary where one exists, and from the labels in the dashboard's own code. Every link was checked against the dashboard's page routes. It covers topics and variables.
